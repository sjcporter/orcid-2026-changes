Last time I looked at one university's ORCID integration. This time, all of New South Wales.

Ten NSW universities now write to their researchers' ORCID records, from New England and Wollongong in 2016 to Western Sydney this year. I followed the same 23.6k NSW researchers through the 2024, 2025 and 2026 public data files.

🔗 Co-author links between them: 81.9k → 124k (+51%)
🆕 2 in 5 of the new links are visible only because a university's own system sent the work
🏛️ Links between two different NSW universities: +46%, with UNSW–Sydney up from 6.5k to 10.0k

The biggest single change was the University of Sydney's Profile Manager. In its first year it made 4.4k of the 26.1k co-author links that appeared across NSW visible: 1 in 6 of the state's new links, and half of them connect Sydney researchers to colleagues at other NSW universities.

Reach varies a lot. Macquarie and Charles Sturt write to about four in five of their research staff on ORCID. UNSW and Sydney, the two biggest, reach about one in seven so far.

The same gap as last time shows up across the state: works are automated, affiliations mostly aren't. Between publishers, Crossref and the universities, 85–95% of new works arrive without the researcher typing anything. But the employer, the assertion the university is best placed to make, is written by the university for 63–71% of staff at Charles Sturt, Macquarie and Southern Cross, about half at UNSW (which bulk-loaded 2.8k this year), and almost none at Sydney or Western Sydney.

If every NSW university reached four in five of its researchers, how much more of the state's research network would we see?

(Method: research staff = ORCID records with a current employment entry at the university, at least one DOI work and no student role. A link = two researchers sharing a DOI; papers with more than 100 ORCID iDs left out. Public data only, so all counts are lower bounds.)
